## 1. Introduction and Architectural Motivation

Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Networks (DePIN) depend upon reliable assertions regarding the state and dynamics of the physical universe. Existing architectures exhibit an epistemological flaw: they conflate cryptographic message authenticity with empirical reality. A valid digital signature guarantees only that a specific private key produced an assertion; it provides no evidence that the asserted physical phenomenon occurred, that the sensing device operated within valid physical parameters, or that the physical environment was not manipulated.

The Physical Evidence Consensus (PEC) protocol provides a formal, deterministic framework for verifying physical observations across distributed networks. Adopting the architecture defined by the IETF Remote ATtestation ProcedureS (RATS, RFC 9334) standard, PEC establishes a deterministic evaluation pipeline transforming untrusted sensor observations into mathematically bounded, verified physical state transitions.

## 2. Epistemological Taxonomy

To prevent category errors in verification logic, PEC strictly formalizes four distinct ontological categories:

```text
+---------------------+     +--------------------+     +------------------+     +------------------+
| Hardware Attestation| --> | Sensor Observation | --> |  Physical Event  | --> |  Physical Claim  |
+---------------------+     +--------------------+     +------------------+     +------------------+
  Silicon State &             Transducer Voltage &       Localized Spatial-       Synthesized Assert-
  Key Integrity               Quantized Telemetry        Temporal Dynamic         ion for Settlement
```

1. **Hardware Attestation**: Cryptographic proof of the internal execution state, firmware digest, and silicon identity of the collecting device (for example, via Physical Unclonable Functions or Secure Elements).
2. **Sensor Observation**: Direct digital or analog output produced by a transducer converting an environmental condition into quantized telemetry.
3. **Physical Event**: An actual thermodynamic, kinematic, or electromagnetic occurrence localized in a specific space-time coordinate $(x, y, z, t)$.
4. **Physical Claim**: A structured proposition asserted by an agent regarding a Physical Event, submitted for decentralized verification and settlement.

### The Non-Equivalence Axiom

All verification algorithms in PEC must enforce the Non-Equivalence Axiom:

$$\text{Measurement Integrity} \not\equiv \text{Measurement Validity} \not\equiv \text{Measurement Plausibility} \not\equiv \text{Physical Truth}$$

* **Measurement Integrity**: The bitstream was generated by an authenticated private key and was not altered in transit.
* **Measurement Validity**: The observation was recorded by an intact transducer operating within its certified calibration envelope and sampling frequency bounds.
* **Measurement Plausibility**: The observation obeys localized physics conservation laws and correlates with adjacent, independent sensor streams.
* **Physical Truth**: The objective state of the physical environment, which can only be asymptotically approximated through bounded uncertainty intervals.

## 3. Observation Authority Envelope ($A_{obs}$)

A transducer cannot assert arbitrary physical quantities across unbounded domains. An observation is admitted to the verification pipeline only if it falls within the certified Observation Authority Envelope $A_{obs}$ defined as:

$$A_{obs} = \langle \mathcal{D}_{range}, \mathcal{R}_{slew}, f_{sample}, \vec{C}_{calib}, U_{std}, T_{cert} \rangle$$

Where:
* $\mathcal{D}_{range} = [v_{min}, v_{max}]$: Admissible dynamic measurement range. Values outside this interval trigger instant hardware fault classification.
* $\mathcal{R}_{slew} = \left| \frac{dv}{dt} \right|_{max}$: Maximum physically possible rate of change based on transducer time constants.
* $f_{sample} = [f_{min}, f_{max}]$: Required Nyquist sampling frequency bounds.
* $\vec{C}_{calib}$: Cryptographically committed polynomial coefficients mapping raw transducer output to SI units.
* $U_{std}$: Standard expanded measurement uncertainty evaluated under ISO/IEC Guide 98-3 (GUM).
* $T_{cert}$: Temporal validity window for current factory or peer-recalibrated certificate.

Telemetry received from any device asserting values outside $A_{obs}$ is rejected deterministically during phase 1 verification without invoking graph solver resources.

## 5. Physical Evidence Hypergraph (PEH)

The central data structure of the verification pipeline is the Physical Evidence Hypergraph:

$$\mathcal{G}_{PEH} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$$

### 5.1 Typed Vertices ($\mathcal{V}$)

Vertices represent discrete epistemological artifacts:
1. `OBSERVATION`: Raw or preprocessed transducer readings signed by an Attester.
2. `ATTESTATION`: Hardware identity, firmware digests, and physical environment proofs.
3. `REFERENCE`: Known baseline values (for example, standard gravity $g_0$, speed of light $c$, sea-level pressure).
4. `MODEL`: Deterministic transfer functions, differential equation solvers, and thermodynamic relations.
5. `CONTEXT`: Spatiotemporal tags, local topology, solar zenith angles, and weather models.
6. `CONSTRAINT`: Strict bounds derived from physics conservation principles.
7. `CONSEQUENCE`: Logical deductions generated through rule evaluation.
8. `VERIFIER_ASSERTION`: Intermediate verification attestations signed by PEC-VM execution nodes.

### 5.2 Directed Hyperedge Relations ($\mathcal{E}$)

Hyperedges connect subsets of vertices representing causal and verification relationships:
* `SUPPORTS` $(V_{src} \to v_{dst})$: The source vertex set increases the empirical likelihood of $v_{dst}$.
* `CONTRADICTS` $(V_{src} \longleftrightarrow v_{dst})$: Mutual exclusion between vertex sets violating bounds.
* `CONDITIONS` $(V_{src} \to v_{dst})$: $v_{dst}$ is semantically meaningful only if $V_{src}$ is formally verified.
* `DERIVES` $(V_{src} \to v_{dst})$: $v_{dst}$ is computed deterministically from $V_{src}$ via an evaluated `MODEL`.
* `DEPENDS_ON` $(V_{src} \to v_{dst})$: Identifies causal, architectural, or operational coupling.

### 5.3 Deterministic Conflict Resolution

When a non-empty Contradiction Set $CS = \{e \in \mathcal{E} \mid e.\text{type} = \text{CONTRADICTS}\}$ occurs, the PEC-VM executes deterministic resolution:

```text
Algorithm: ResolveContradictionSet(CS, PEH)
Input: Contradiction Set CS, Hypergraph PEH
Output: Pruned PEH, Updated Contested Coordinates

1. For each hyperedge e in CS:
2.    Identify disjoint subgraphs G_A and G_B joined by e.
3.    Compute effective independence count: N_eff(G_A) and N_eff(G_B).
4.    Compute cumulative hardware assurance depth: PA(G_A) and PA(G_B).
5.    Delta_Confidence = | (N_eff(G_A) * PA(G_A)) - (N_eff(G_B) * PA(G_B)) |
6.    If Delta_Confidence >= THRESHOLD_RESOLUTION:
7.        Prune subgraph with lower metric to state INVALIDated.
8.        Mark surviving subgraph as VALIDated with confidence penalty.
9.    Else:
10.       Flag all vertices in G_A union G_B with DecisionState = CONTESTED.
11.       Escalate to active challenge-response or dispute bond slashing.
```

## 6. Dependency Tensor and Effective Evidence Count ($N_{eff}$)

Multiple sensor readings claiming agreement cannot be treated as statistically independent if they share failure modes, common firmware, or proximate physical positioning.

### 6.1 Decomposed Dependency Matrix

The aggregate pairwise dependency $D_{ij} \in [0, 1]$ between evidence sources $i$ and $j$ is computed as:

$$D_{ij} = w_p D_{physical}(i, j) + w_a D_{arch}(i, j) + w_o D_{op}(i, j) + w_{adv} D_{adv}(i, j)$$

Subject to $\sum w = 1.0$, where:
* $D_{physical}(i, j)$: Spatial and environmental correlation based on sensor separation distance $d(i, j)$ and physical dispersion models:
  $$D_{physical}(i, j) = \exp\left(-\frac{d(i, j)^2}{2\sigma_d^2}\right)$$
* $D_{arch}(i, j)$: Common mode failure metric across hardware supply chain, microcontroller SKU, sensor model, and firmware version hash.
* $D_{op}(i, j)$: Operational ownership overlap (for example, shared operator staking key, shared power grid, or common network AS route).
* $D_{adv}(i, j)$: Collusion incentive factor based on shared financial beneficiaries in downstream smart contracts.

### 6.2 Effective Independence Count ($N_{eff}$)

Given $M$ raw observations with cross-dependency matrix $D \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M}$, the dependency-weighted effective evidence count $N_{eff}$ is calculated deterministically via eigenvalue decomposition:

$$N_{eff} = \frac{\left(\sum_{k=1}^M \lambda_k\right)^2}{\sum_{k=1}^M \lambda_k^2} = \frac{(\text{Tr}(D))^2}{\text{Tr}(D^2)} = \frac{M^2}{\sum_{i=1}^M \sum_{j=1}^M D_{ij}^2}$$

Where $\lambda_k$ are the eigenvalues of $D$. When all sources are completely coupled ($D_{ij} = 1$), $N_{eff} = 1.0$. When all sources are completely orthogonal ($D_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$), $N_{eff} = M$.

## 7. Physics Invariants and Proof-of-Physical-Work

PEC validates claims by testing them against an invariant hierarchy grounded in fundamental physics conservation laws.

### 7.1 Invariant Hierarchy ($I_0 \to I_6$)

1. **$I_0$: Thermodynamic Conservation**: Energy continuity across system boundaries. Enforces $\Delta U = Q - W$.
2. **$I_1$: Mass and Continuity Conservation**: Fluid and particulate flow continuity: $\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{v}) = 0$.
3. **$I_2$: Kinematic and Momentum Conservation**: Maximum acceleration, velocity bounds, and ballistic trajectories: $\sum \vec{F} = m \vec{a}$.
4. **$I_3$: Electromagnetic Attenuation Limits**: Friis transmission equation and free-space path loss limits for RF telemetry:
   $$P_r = P_t G_t G_r \left(\frac{\lambda}{4\pi d}\right)^2$$
5. **$I_4$: Spatial Correlation and Diffusion**: Gradient limits for environmental variables (for example, temperature, pressure, particulate density) modeled by the heat/diffusion equation:
   $$\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} = \alpha \nabla^2 \phi$$
6. **$I_5$: Monotonic Entropy Production**: Physical processes must demonstrate non-negative entropy generation: $\Delta S_{universe} \ge 0$.
7. **$I_6$: Temporal Causality and Relativistic Invariance**: No signal or causal influence can propagate faster than the speed of light $c$: $|\Delta \vec{x}| \le c \Delta t$.

### 7.2 Dynamic Tolerance Equation ($\epsilon_E$)

A physical claim claiming consistency with an invariant model $M_{inv}$ must satisfy:

$$|v_{observed} - M_{inv}(\vec{x}_{context})| \le \epsilon_E$$

Where the dynamic tolerance threshold $\epsilon_E$ is computed as:

$$\epsilon_E = k_e \sqrt{U_{transducer}^2 + U_{model}^2 + U_{env}^2} + \kappa_{drift}(t - t_0)$$

Where:
* $k_e$: Coverage factor (typically $k_e = 3$ for $99.7\%$ empirical confidence).
* $U_{transducer}$: Certified standard uncertainty from $A_{obs}$.
* $U_{model}$: Bounded numerical approximation error of the deterministic model.
* $U_{env}$: Microclimate variance parameter derived from regional context vertices.
* $\kappa_{drift}$: Time-dependent drift coefficient per unit time since last calibration epoch $t_0$.

### 7.3 Useful Physical Work Fraction ($\eta_{useful}$)

For Proof-of-Physical-Work (PoPW), the useful physical work fraction $\eta_{useful}$ distinguishes productive physical service from waste heat:

$$\eta_{useful} = \frac{W_{verified}}{E_{total}} = \frac{\int_{t_0}^{t_1} \vec{F}_{useful} \cdot d\vec{x} + \int_{t_0}^{t_1} P_{service} dt}{E_{input}}$$

Claims are rewarded proportionally to $\eta_{useful}$ only when the denominator $E_{input}$ is verified by independent invariant cross-checks.

## 8. Active Challenge-Response Protocols

When passive verification yields an `UNKNOWN` or `CONTESTED` state, the PEC protocol initiates an active Challenge-Response routine.

### 8.1 Actuation Safety Classes

Active stimulus carries physical risk. Actuators are categorized into strict safety classes:

* **$C_0$: Zero Physical Risk**: Purely electromagnetic, computational, or passive optical challenge (for example, pseudo-random LED blinking, frequency shift keying).
* **$C_1$: Low Physical Risk**: Sub-threshold mechanical movement or thermal variance imperceptible to humans and harmless to machinery (for example, micro-vibration sweep, $\Delta T \le 0.5$ K).
* **$C_2$: Moderate Physical Risk**: Controlled mechanical actuation requiring system interlocks and operator confirmation (for example, valve throttling, robotic arm deflection).
* **$C_3$: Critical Physical Risk**: High-energy actuation strictly forbidden from autonomous network dispatch without multi-signature cryptographic hardware lockout.

### 8.2 Proof-of-Stimulus ($PoS$) and Proof-of-Response ($PoR$)

1. **Challenge Generation**: The PEC-VM selects an unpredictable nonce $N_{ch}$ derived from the latest canonical block hash and generates an actuation command $Cmd(N_{ch})$ within safety class $C_k \le C_{threshold}$.
2. **Proof-of-Stimulus ($PoS$)**: The stimulating node executes the command and signs an attestation containing the precise timestamp $t_s$, electrical energy consumed $\Delta E_s$, and the command digest:
   $$PoS = \text{Sign}_{SK_{act}}(\text{SHA256}(Cmd(N_{ch}) \parallel t_s \parallel \Delta E_s))$$
3. **Proof-of-Response ($PoR$)**: Surrounding observer nodes monitor the physical environment for the expected physical perturbation $\Delta \Phi_{predicted}(Cmd(N_{ch}))$. Observers submit:
   $$PoR_i = \text{Sign}_{SK_i}(\text{SHA256}(PoS \parallel \Delta \Phi_{measured} \parallel t_r))$$
4. **Verification**: The claim is validated if and only if:
   $$t_r - t_s \in [\tau_{propagation\_min}, \tau_{propagation\_max}]$$
   $$\|\Delta \Phi_{measured} - \Delta \Phi_{predicted}\| \le \epsilon_{stimulus}$$

## 9. Deterministic Mathematics Profile (PEC-MATH-01)

Floating-point operations are non-deterministic across heterogeneous CPU architectures (IEEE 754 allows variance in transcendentals, rounding modes, and FMA execution). All mathematical evaluations within PEC-VM adhere strictly to the `PEC-MATH-01` profile.

### 9.1 Data Representation

All fractional calculations use signed 128-bit fixed-point representation with 64 integer bits and 64 fractional bits (Q64.64):

```text
 127 126                           64 63                            0
+---+-------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| S |     Integer Part (63 bits)    |    Fractional Part (64 bits)   |
+---+-------------------------------+--------------------------------+
```

* Value representation: $X = x \cdot 2^{-64}$, where $x \in [-2^{127}, 2^{127}-1]$.
* Minimum resolution: $2^{-64} \approx 5.421 \times 10^{-20}$.
* Maximum magnitude: $\approx 9.223 \times 10^{18}$.

### 9.2 Arithmetic Rules

1. **Saturation on Overflow**: Arithmetic operations must saturate at maximum or minimum allowable values rather than wrapping modulo $2^{128}$.
2. **Convergent Rounding**: Half-way values round to the nearest even integer (banker's rounding) to eliminate positive or negative bias accumulation in iterative integration.
3. **Division Algorithm**: Deterministic unsigned restoring division with explicit remainder tracking followed by signed two's-complement conversion.

### 9.3 Transcendental Functions via CORDIC

Trigonometric, hyperbolic, and exponential functions are calculated using fixed-point CORDIC (Coordinate Rotation Digital Computer) algorithms executed for exactly 64 iterations:

```text
Angle Accumulator:  z_{k+1} = z_k - d_k * arctan(2^{-k})
X Coordinate:       x_{k+1} = x_k - d_k * y_k * 2^{-k}
Y Coordinate:       y_{k+1} = y_k + d_k * x_k * 2^{-k}
Where d_k = -1 if z_k < 0 else +1
```

Precomputed 64-entry lookup tables for $\arctan(2^{-k})$ and $2^{-k}$ are hardcoded in the protocol specification to ensure identical cross-platform state transitions.

## 10. Formal Security Bounds and Non-Goals

### 10.1 Conditional Soundness ($\epsilon_s$)

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an active adversary controlling up to fraction $\alpha_{byz}$ of network nodes and capable of physical sensor spoofing at cost function $C_{spoof}(\vec{x})$. The probability of an invalid physical claim being classified as `VALID` is bounded by:

$$P(\text{DecisionState} = \text{VALID} \mid \text{Physical Reality} = \text{FALSE}) \le \epsilon_s$$

Where:

$$\epsilon_s = \prod_{k=0}^6 \left(1 - P(I_k \text{ detects violation})\right) \cdot \exp\left(-\gamma \cdot N_{eff}\right) + P_{tamper}(\text{RoT})$$

Where $\gamma$ is the cross-sensor discrimination coefficient and $P_{tamper}(\text{RoT})$ is the silicon attack failure probability.

### 10.2 Conditional Completeness ($\epsilon_c$)

The probability that a genuine physical event is mistakenly rejected is bounded by:

$$P(\text{DecisionState} = \text{INVALID} \mid \text{Physical Reality} = \text{TRUE}) \le \epsilon_c$$

$$\epsilon_c \le \text{erfc}\left(\frac{\epsilon_E - \mu_{noise}}{\sigma_{noise} \sqrt{2}}\right) + P_{drop}(\text{P2P})$$

Ensuring that sufficiently calibrated sensors under standard noise regimes achieve deterministic acceptance.

### 10.3 Protocol Non-Goals

To maintain rigorous architectural boundaries, PEC explicitly defines the following non-goals:

1. **Universal Oracle Equivalence**: PEC does not attempt to resolve subjective, human-defined, or non-quantifiable assertions (for example, visual aesthetic quality or legal ownership).
2. **Absolute Defense Against Perfect Physical Simulation**: If an adversary completely encloses a transducer in an airtight, thermal-regulated, electro-magnetic shielded chamber providing a perfect, continuous, multi-modal simulation of physical reality at infinite cost, PEC cannot differentiate this from physical reality without external unshielded witnesses.
3. **Low-Latency Soft Real-Time Guarantees**: PEC optimizes for mathematical determinism and epistemological certainty over sub-millisecond settlement. Final settlement coordinates require multi-source hypergraph stabilization.
